Peckworks JobKit

Welcome. Here is your first day.

JobKit is a job-search workspace that lives entirely on your computer. You talk to Claude in plain English; JobKit keeps the folders, the tracking, and this dashboard organized for you. Nothing about you or your search is ever uploaded anywhere.

1Put your resume in the Baseline folder

Inside your workspace there is a folder called Baseline. Save your current resume there (any format is fine, plain text works great). If you do not have one, just tell Claude and it will build one with you, one job at a time.

Why it matters: every resume and cover letter JobKit ever writes is built only from what is in Baseline. It never invents experience for you. On team projects, be exact about which part was yours, because that is exactly what will be claimed later.

2Paste a job link

Found a job posting? Copy the link, come back to Claude, and say:

Here is a job: https://example.com/careers/3d-artist

JobKit reads the posting, scores it 1 to 10 against what you told it you want, and files it in a tracked folder. If a site will not let it in, paste the posting text instead; that always works.

3Tell Claude what happens

You drive; JobKit never guesses. When something changes, just say it like you would to a friend:

I applied to the LumenForge job. BrightPath rejected me. I have an interview at Marrowfield on Thursday.

Silence is never treated as rejection, and dates are only recorded when you report them. The dashboard updates itself after every change.

4Your dashboard

Double-click CareerDashboard.html in your workspace folder. Bookmark it. It is a plain file, no login, no server, works offline. It shows what is ready to apply to, what you are waiting on, interviews, offers, and this library.

5The library: pages that teach you things

The Library section of the dashboard shows every page in your guides folder, including this one. Want to actually learn something for this search?

Make me a study page about salary negotiation.

Claude builds it, drops it into guides, and it appears in the library with an icon and a category. If read tracking is on, pages you have opened get marked as read, so you can see what you have actually studied. (Read tracking works with Chrome-family browsers: Chrome, Brave, Edge. Safari keeps its history private, so pages opened there stay unmarked.)

6Make it yours

Want JobKit to behave differently? You never have to edit the tool. Just tell Claude:

Remember this: never show me unpaid or "for exposure" postings.

That gets written into the CLAUDE.md file in your workspace, and every JobKit skill obeys that file over its own defaults. Add steps to the workflow, change the score cutoff, rename things, whatever you want. JobKit also writes its own dated lessons there as it learns your search; that file is yours to edit or delete.

Everything stays on your computer. The only outbound request on any JobKit page is for the two fonts, and it works fine without them.
